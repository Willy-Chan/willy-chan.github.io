*Note*: This is part of a series of personal notes on topics I'm trying to learn more deeply. My working hypothesis is that teaching something is one of the best ways to learn it — explaining forces you to confront the gaps, and you notice immediately when you're hand-waving.

Each post has two parts: a short set of "teaching" notes (the order I'd introduce the ideas), then my handwritten notes (the main content). In my experience, having both matter a lot.

## "Teaching" Notes:

Here's my opinionated take on how to teach beginners about SSMs. The full PDF is at the bottom of this page.

The flow I'd use:

- **Pitch.** Why care about SSMs at all? Attention is quadratic in sequence length; an SSM step doesn't grow with length.

- **Mental model.** The S4 Hazy Research post frames SSMs as having "3 forms," but they all reduce to one simple recurrence. I show that recurrence a few ways — RNN diagram, computation graph, expanding the equations — hoping at least one lands.

- **Worked examples.** Plug in small vectors and matrices and grind through the computation by hand. That does more for building intuition about shapes and mechanics than another round of abstractions.

- **Convolution reveal at the end.** Once the recurrence clicks, expand and substitute: the convolutional form falls out of what you already know. That was the most satisfying bit for me.
