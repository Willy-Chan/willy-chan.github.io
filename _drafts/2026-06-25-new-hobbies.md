title: Is Life a Series of Geometric Random Variables?
math: true
---

A lot of advice about working hard treats effort like a virtue: grind because it's character-building, or because suffering is somehow the point. I find that unconvincing. The better reason is simpler and more mathematical.

A lot of what matters only has to succeed once: one job offer, one paper accepted, one concept that finally clicks. Fail ninety-nine times and the hundredth still counts as winning.

That's just a geometric distribution. If each attempt succeeds independently with probability p, the expected number of tries until the first success is 1/p. So if p = 0.01, expect about 100 attempts. If p = 0.0001, about 10,000. Failure isn't proof you're doomed, it's often just what 1/p looks like while you're still counting.

p isn't the same for everyone. Talent, timing, network, luck: some people's dice are just more loaded than others. It's not fair, but that's the way the world is, and that explains a lot of "effortless" success. But it doesn't change the core point. Unless p = 1, success isn't free. You still have to try, and you still have to fail, before you get a hit.

So I'll keep defending working hard. Not because effort is noble; it's because if you only need one success, volume is how you buy it.
